01What we were solving for

Numbers are relative to the current prototype layup, re-solved on this model at the corrected ply thickness.

The prototype ply book was built from fabrics no longer in stock. Re-solved at the corrected cured thickness (§2) it lands at 11.96 N flex, mid kick (f 0.46), k_twist 12 774 N·mm/rad at a probe tip angle of 118°. That is the baseline every candidate is measured against.

  • Flex — the equivalent hung weight W at a 90° tip tangent on the buckling bench. Target: baseline − 2 N ≈ 10 N, trade band ±2 N.
  • Kick point — span fraction of peak curvature, bucketed heel / mid / tip. Target: mid, low migration with load.
  • k_twist — a comparative torsional-stiffness number from a small force-couple probe on the buckled blade. Larger is better. Only comparable between layups that reach a similar probe tip angle; headline ratios are quoted at a re-matched θ ≈ 118°.
  • Buildability — no biax purchase; a stack that cures flat (B ≈ 0).

02Two model changes, both committed

On branch exp/nextgen-fin. The solving regression gates still pass.

Cured ply thickness × 1.1

The built prototype measured ~1.2× the dry gsm/1000 rule; 1.1 is the adopted compromise — part real ply loft, part resin. CURED_PLY_FACTOR = 1.1 scales the derived thickness only. The card moduli are not scaled with it, so since flex ~ E·t³ the factor moves stiffness directly — a full resin-pickup model would also drop E₁ ~1.2×. Going 1.0 → 1.1 lifts the modelled prototype flex ~9 → 12 N; 1.2 gave ~15.5 N. Absolute newtons will firm up with a per-fabric micrometer + Vf pass.

UD fabric is intermediate-modulus

The in-stock Hexcel UD is an IM fibre roll (~43 Msi, "IM446"), not the standard-modulus card the library assumed. hexcel_uni_231 now carries the vetted IM lamina constants (E₁ 121 → 153 GPa, reused from hexcel_im2_uni_193). The 3k twill stays SM AS4C. The baseline is unaffected — its ply book uses IM2/IM7, not hexcel_uni_231.

03How the search went

About 120 CalculiX solves across four parametric sweeps plus the hand-written finalists. The method — the loop, the levers, the B rule, the report generator — is packaged as the repo skill .claude/skills/fin-layup-design/ so it stays out of CLAUDE.md.

  • Symmetric parametric sweeps. A generated symmetric taper forces every zone pad to a mirrored pair of plies — its smallest mid-span pad is 2 plies, a ~+8 N flex lever, so no symmetric design hit the soft target and a mid kick. A light QUARTER pad + a thin through-section moved every design to a clean mid kick, but the flex quantum (one UD ply) leaves a gap where the target sits.
  • ±45° skin architecture. Full ±45 twill skins over a UD bending core: the shell carries torsion, the core carries flex. k_twist ~2.4× a 0/90-skinned blade at a matched θ, for little flex cost. The thinner biax ±45 skin was too light to carry torsion.
  • Asymmetric single-ply taper (the ax* finalists). Hand-written ply books — ±45 twill skins, UD core, single-ply zone steps — fill the flex gap and land the target. Ply order then interleaved for B ≈ 0.

07What B is, and why every finalist is interleaved

B is the term in laminate theory that couples in-plane load to bending. It is the only warning this model gives that the cured part will not match the solved shape.

The plate response is [N; M] = [A B; B D][ε₀; κ]. A (stretch) and D (bend) are always present; B couples them, so a pure in-plane force produces curvature and vice versa. B is zero only when the stack mirrors itself about the mid-plane in both ply angle and ply material — any front/back mismatch leaves a residual.

For a fin it matters two ways: (1) cure shrinkage is an in-plane load, so through B the blade comes off the single-sided mould warped — and this model has no cure step, so B is the only signal; (2) extension–bending coupling muddies the flex reading. A useful index is B / √(A₁₁·D₁₁): < 0.03 cures flat, 0.05–0.15 tolerable (the shipped prototype sits here), > 0.2 visibly warps.

An asymmetric single-ply taper starts with a poor B because listing all FULL plies, then all MID, then all QUARTER clusters the inboard-zone plies on one face. Interleaving reorders them so each zone's stack is a palindrome: ±45 skins on both faces, 0° plies in even count and a single material, an odd centre ply on the mid-plane. ax2 → ax2r took the root from [45/0/0/0/0/45/45] to [45/0/0/45/0/0/45], same plies; flex moved ~0.3 N, kick unchanged. ax3 → ax3r moved ~1.5 N because its ±45 plies travelled further to the faces — the interleave is not always a small move; re-solve it.

One irreducible case: a twill@0 ply and a UD@0 ply in the same stack differ in stiffness and thickness at the two “0” positions — no reordering makes them mirror. ax5r keeps its twill@0 softener and holds a ~0.19 residual; ax3r drops it (all-UD) and reaches zero. Pick one 0° material, or go single-fabric like ax0.

08Biax: does the purchase earn its place?

No — not with the fabric data we have.

In the library, hexcel_himax_biax_100 is a stand-in card: its stiffness constants are byte-identical to the 3k twill, and it has no ±45 baked in (the angle comes from the ply book). So today, biax is simply a thinner twill — a 0.11 mm ply instead of 0.22 mm.

ax2rb — biax ±45 skin

9 plies  ·  buy biax

The thinner ±45 shell carries less torsion, so ax2rb needs an extra UD ply to hold flex — which overshoots to 19.5 N and drops the twist ratio to 1.34×. Biax makes the fin worse on every axis here. A real measured HiMax ±45 lamina card could change this (a genuine ±45 architecture has ~5–10× the in-plane shear of a rotated 0/90 woven); until that card exists there is no case to buy the fabric.

10Caveats & what's next

  • Absolute newtons will move. FIN_TEST_3.step is not dimensionally final (flex ~ thickness³ ~ length³) and the ×1.1 cured factor is a modulus-free compromise. A per-fabric micrometer + Vf pass would firm the absolute numbers; the ratios are the deliverable.
  • k_twist is comparative and θ-sensitive. Ratios are quoted at a re-matched drive where the probe tip angle is ~118°. Any layup compared later must be re-probed to a matched θ. The ×1.1 / ×1.2 factor change moved every absolute flex ~23 % but left the k_twist ratios unchanged.
  • Not modelled: blade self-weight (~2 N in the compression path), residual cure stress, double curvature. The B ≈ 0 result is the only guard against mould warp.
  • Superseded: the un-interleaved ax1/ax3/ax5 first-pass books — build from ax0 / ax5r / ax3r / ax2r / ax2r1.
  • Open: commit the finalists + NEXTGEN.md to exp/nextgen-fin (done); a per-fabric cured-thickness measurement is the next input.
